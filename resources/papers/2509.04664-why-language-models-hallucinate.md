## One-Sentence Memory

Hallucinations are not mysterious failures but the predictable statistical outcome of how LLMs are trained and evaluated: pretraining pressures produce errors even on clean data, and mainstream benchmarks that grade right = 1 / wrong-or-blank = 0 reward confident guessing over admitting uncertainty — so the fix is socio-technical (change how the dominant benchmarks score), not another hallucination benchmark.

## Two Causes

### 1. Training-pipeline statistics (why hallucinations originate)

- Hallucinations begin as errors in a **binary classification** problem during pretraining: distinguishing valid from invalid statements ("Is-It-Valid").
- If incorrect statements cannot be statistically separated from facts in the training distribution, generation errors arise from **natural statistical pressure** — even with perfect data and infinite compute.
- **Generation–Classification Inequality** (the key result): generative error rate ≥ 2 × (Is-It-Valid) classification error rate, minus a calibration term. I.e. **generating correct text is fundamentally harder than verifying whether text is correct.**
- Facts with no learnable pattern and low frequency (e.g. rare "tail" birthdays) are especially prone: the model has nothing to generalize from, so it guesses.

### 2. Evaluation incentives (why hallucinations persist)

- Most benchmarks and leaderboards **penalize uncertainty**: a confident wrong answer scores the same as a blank (0), while a confident correct guess scores 1.
- Under that rule, **guessing strictly dominates abstaining** (positive expected value vs zero), so models optimized to be good test-takers learn to always answer.
- The authors call this an **"epidemic of penalizing uncertain responses."** Post-training reward models and human graders can also be fooled by plausible-but-false confidence, reinforcing it.

## Why It Persists in SOTA Models

- Training data lacks "I don't know" completions (text looks like "May 27th, 1971", not expressions of uncertainty).
- Reward models / human graders in post-training can be fooled by fluent falsehoods.
- Benchmark leaderboards dominate the field and reward confident answering, not calibration.

## Proposed Solution (socio-technical)

Do **not** add more standalone hallucination evals; instead **rescore the mainstream benchmarks that dominate leaderboards** so incentives align:

- Reward appropriate/expressed uncertainty.
- Penalize confident errors more heavily than uncertain ones (asymmetric scoring).
- Optimize for **calibration**, not just accuracy.
- Use architectural patterns — RAG for factual queries, explicit **confidence thresholds** for high-stakes decisions.
- Make target confidence explicit in the prompt/grading (answer only if confidence > t; wrong penalized, "IDK" neutral).

## Relationship To The Course (Week 08)

- Direct academic backing for **Experiment 7 / abstention-aware scoring (CRAG +1 / 0 / −1)**: "metrics that reward bluffing get bluffing." Changing the scoring rule, not the model, changes honesty.
- **Generation–Classification Inequality** is the unifying floor under several week concepts:
  - justifies the **small-verifier pattern** (MiniCheck/HHEM at ~1/400 cost) — verification is provably cheaper than generation, so a small model checking a big model's claims is information-theoretically sound, not a hack.
  - echoes RAGChecker's bidirectional entailment and Week 07's "recall is the hard half."
- Reinforces the whole-week thesis `argmax_θ E[μ(q,θ)]`: **the metric μ shapes the system θ** — choose an accuracy-only metric and you train a bluffer.
- Connects to **ECE / calibration** (Experiment 2) and **negative rejection** (Experiment 4): the paper's "reward uncertainty" = the course's calibration + abstention instruments.

## Avaloka Application

- Do **not** evaluate or fine-tune Avaloka on plain accuracy — that literally trains it to bluff. Bake **abstention-aware scoring (+1 / 0 / −1**, heavier penalty for high-stakes) into the eval set so "honest I-don't-know" is the scoring-optimal move.
- Pair with ECE + negative-rejection-rate as the honesty dashboard; set an explicit confidence threshold for high-stakes Care Card decisions.
- Lean on the Generation–Classification asymmetry: use a cheap dedicated verifier on every response, reserve expensive judges for sampled deep checks.
